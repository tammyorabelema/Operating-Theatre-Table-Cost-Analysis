# Five-Year Financial Analysis of Operating Theatre Tables

## Project Overview

This project uses Python to evaluate the estimated five-year cost of ownership of two categories of operating theatre tables:

- **Table A:** Budget imported operating theatre table.
- **Table B:** Premium operating theatre table.

The analysis investigates how initial purchase price, equipment lifespan, preventive maintenance, repair expenditure and operational downtime influence the long-term financial implications of medical equipment procurement.

The objective is to demonstrate how data analysis and financial modelling can inform healthcare technology management and medical equipment purchasing decisions.

## Project Objectives

- Compare the purchase costs of two operating theatre tables.
- Estimate replacement expenditure over five years.
- Examine maintenance and repair costs.
- Estimate the financial impact of equipment downtime.
- Calculate five-year Total Cost of Ownership (TCO).
- Compare best-case and worst-case financial scenarios.
- Present findings using Python-generated tables and visualisations.

## Tools and Technologies

- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Jupyter Notebook

## Methodology

A five-year financial model was developed using two operating theatre table profiles.

The model considers:

1. Initial purchase and replacement expenditure.
2. Estimated equipment lifespan.
3. Annual preventive maintenance costs.
4. Annual repair costs.
5. Estimated downtime and associated financial impact.

Two replacement scenarios were considered:

- **Best-case scenario:** Based on the longer estimated equipment lifespan.
- **Worst-case scenario:** Based on the shorter estimated equipment lifespan.

Python was used to organise the input data, calculate expenditure, compare scenarios, generate visualisations and export the results.

## Preliminary Findings

Under the illustrative assumptions used, the budget table has a lower estimated five-year ownership cost than the premium table.

However, the premium table has lower assumed maintenance, repair and downtime expenses.

This demonstrates that a lower purchase price does not necessarily indicate lower maintenance expenditure, while a higher purchase price does not automatically guarantee better long-term financial value.

## Limitations

This is a scenario-based financial analysis rather than an empirical study of hospital equipment performance.

Some input figures are based on available equipment pricing and estimated lifespan information, while maintenance, repair and downtime costs are illustrative assumptions.

The analysis does not account for:

- Inflation and changes in currency value.
- Financing and procurement costs.
- Residual equipment value.
- Independently verified failure rates.
- Differences in clinical performance.
- Actual hospital utilisation and service records.

The results should therefore be interpreted as financial scenarios, not verified predictions or evidence that one category of operating table is clinically superior.

## Conclusion

The project demonstrates the application of Python-based financial modelling to medical equipment procurement.

By considering purchase expenditure alongside maintenance, repairs, equipment lifespan and downtime, healthcare organisations can develop a more comprehensive understanding of the potential long-term costs associated with medical equipment ownership.

## Project Status

Completed initial financial modelling and scenario comparison. Further improvements could include sensitivity analysis, inflation adjustment and the incorporation of verified hospital maintenance records.
